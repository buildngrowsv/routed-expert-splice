# Does the recipient need a different early feature vector for each prompt?

After ten blocks of the 7B donor, the fitted feature map matched the full graft's 166 complete repairs. It beat a map fitted with shuffled targets by only 23 groups and missed the registered behavioral gate. Both affine fits preserve the mean from the decoy calibration data. A fixed feature vector might explain part or all of the improvement over dropping the donor. [Early-exit comparison](frozen_graft_early_exit_study/README.md)

Use the same 512 executable held-out triplets, frozen 14B host and frozen learned bridge. Recompute aligned early-feature scores exactly. Compare two interventions on the saved early features: supply the same calibration-target mean to every prompt, or cyclically substitute the next member's source within each matched triplet. The mean preserves the fitted intercept and removes prompt dependence. The cyclic test keeps the three feature vectors and their distribution fixed while changing which opener produced each. Retain the earlier full-teacher, matched host, shuffled-fit and zero-feature controls. Fit no new model weights and use no extra donor forward passes for the new comparison.

Use 5,000 paired group resamples. The prompt-specific arm needs at least five points over the constant with positive lower bound, and at least ten complete groups shared with the teacher and missed by the constant, cyclic, shuffled, host and ablated controls. The constant qualifies as a substitute if the upper bound on aligned-minus-constant is at most two points and the constant beats the host-feature bridge by at least five points with positive lower bound. Otherwise remain unresolved. Complete following under cyclic substitution is reported separately. The intervention uses the archived tasks and leaves unfamiliar prompt distributions open.
