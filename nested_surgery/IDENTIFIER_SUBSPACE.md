# Can a learned 25-dimensional subspace carry the identifier answer?

In the frozen 7B and 14B Qwen checkpoints, either contiguous one-eighth slice decoded every letter. Swapping either slice reversed zero answers, while swapping the entire source token reversed every pair. Test whether the failure is specific to the coordinate slices. Fit one 25-dimensional subspace to paired activation changes and exchange only the projection of the partner-minus-original vector onto that subspace. All transformer weights stay frozen. [Earlier results](identifier_baseline_study/README.md)

Reuse the 512 token-aligned calibration pairs (generator indices 0–511) and the original block-6 and block-10 activation sites. Fit the per-letter change directions by regressing paired state differences on paired one-hot letter differences. Use the leading 25 orthonormal directions of the fitted class matrix. The maximum number of independent contrasts of 26 letters is 25. The control uses 25 random directions orthogonal to the fitted subspace and scales each injected vector to the same norm as its learned counterpart. This compares the direction of causal edits at the same dose. Both interventions use the same changed source token as the earlier paper. Retain intact and whole-site exchanges. Evaluate 512 fresh pairs at indices 8192–8703 and score reversals of both members among initially correct pairs.

Require at least 100 initially correct pairs and a whole-site reversal rate of at least 20% of those pairs. A useful learned subspace must reverse at least 20% of initially correct pairs and exceed the norm-matched control by at least ten points with a positive lower paired 95% bound. Report partial changes in the two-choice log-odds and both raw counts for both checkpoints. This is a task-supervised causal intervention. Generalizing beyond single-letter completions is a separate question. Expect several minutes based on the prior frozen inference comparison.
