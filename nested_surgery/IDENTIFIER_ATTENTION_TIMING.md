# Can the final token read an edited declaration after block 17?

Permitting only a direct read from the edited declaration to the final token supported 508 of 512 reversals. Blocking that direct edge throughout the remaining layers supported zero, even with other tokens available as relays. The previous block-17 query-token capture also supported zero. Test whether the same direct edge can wait until after block 17, or whether it needs to be available early.

Use 384 new two-class groups at indices 98000–98383. Apply the same 25-direction edit just after block 10 to the earlier declaration. Test four explicit causal-mask schedules. The direct edge is available throughout, available only in blocks 11–17, available only in blocks 18–48, or closed throughout. All other edges stay open under the standard causal mask. Compare each edited run to a clean run under its identical schedule. This experiment gives the late interval more layers and asks a boundary question. It does not identify a critical individual layer.

Require at least 100 groups with all four native answers correct under every schedule, at least 60% reversal on the edited class in the all-open control, at most 15% on the other class in that control, and at most 15% reversal on the edited class with the edge closed throughout. Report each window's paired difference from both the other window and unrestricted. A window is sufficient if it reverses at least 70% of the edited-class queries with a paired lower 95% bound on its difference from unrestricted greater than or equal to minus 15 points. A window leads if its paired advantage has a lower bound above 20 points. Record the other-class reversals separately. The fitted basis, transformer and readout stay frozen. Estimate seven minutes from the full-depth path run.
