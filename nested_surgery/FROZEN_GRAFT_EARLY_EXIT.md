# Can ten donor blocks replace the full 28-block specialist for the frozen graft?

The 14B recipient gained donor-attributable repairs on executable Python with bracket decoys. Its bridge depends on 32 projected coordinates from the frozen 7B specialist's final MLP. Test whether an affine map from the specialist's earlier block-10 residual can reproduce enough of those coordinates for the existing recipient to use them. Fit the map to 32 teacher activations from the same prompt, without answer labels. The experiment follows intermediate-feature distillation and isolates the source of computation because the trained recipient and bridge stay frozen. [Frozen transplant](learned_output_port_decoys_study/README.md) · [FitNets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6550)

Use 192 new decoy triplets to fit one ridge map and 512 disjoint held-out decoy triplets. Hold the same donor and host checkpoints, output bridge, injection site, four candidate vocabulary tokens and three-member exact scoring. Compare to the full teacher feature, a map trained with permuted target rows, the matched host-feature bridge and a zero-feature donor ablation. Also score the intact donor for reference. Use a single prespecified early exit and ridge coefficient of 0.02 times the mean Gram diagonal. Save raw logits, frozen map weights and feature prediction errors.

Require the original graft to solve at least 64 held-out groups. The shallow substitute must retain at least 80% of the original graft's total solves, add at least ten cases shared with the original graft and missed by all matched controls, and beat the shuffled, host-feature and ablated controls by five points with positive lower paired bounds. Even a positive result would cover this constrained synthetic task. It would support computing the 32 interface features after fewer donor blocks. The full donor still runs for the comparison and the independent confidence-routing policy still uses its final logits. [Runner](frozen_graft_early_exit.py)
