# Does the late-copy gap also appear halfway through a separately trained 32B model?

In Qwen2.5-72B, exchanging a late alias token switched zero of 143 matched dictionary groups at block 40, while relocating the original declaration and exchanging its numeral switched 117. Qwen2.5-32B already reliably used the relocated declaration at block 17. Test the same causal contrast at the 32B model's midpoint, block 32 of 64, using the exact underlying Python groups. This is a check across pretrained recipients of unequal size and training history. It cannot isolate parameter count. Hold model weights, task, scoring rule and prompt groups fixed within the 32B comparison. Swap the entire activation of the original numeral in the alias layout, the late alias read, and the numeral in the relocated-definition layout. Count a switch only if the partner's exact number ranks uniquely first over the full vocabulary in both directions and both mirrored contexts. Score constant-output preservation and evaluate the 32B-native intersection across layouts. Also compare absolute outcomes on groups solved natively in both larger-host layouts as a descriptive check on cohort differences.

Require at least 100 shared groups, at least 80% switching and at least 95% constant-control preservation for the relocated definition before interpreting the midpoint. A 95% paired upper confidence bound of −20 points or lower on alias minus relocated replicates the gap. If that criterion misses, an alias with at least 50% switching and 95% preservation is steerable under the 32B checkpoint. Otherwise record unresolved.
