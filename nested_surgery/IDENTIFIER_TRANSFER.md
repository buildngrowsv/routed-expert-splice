# Can an identifier change travel from the 7B coder to the 14B host through 25 dimensions?

The same-model letter experiments reversed every paired answer by exchanging a learned 25-dimensional difference. Test cross-model portability at the declaration token. Fit the two letter subspaces on the registered 512 paired calibration prompts. Fit a regularized linear map from the donor's 25 projected difference coordinates to the host's 25 projected difference coordinates using those same paired prompts. Freeze both transformers, both bases, and the map. [Same-model result](identifier_subspace_study/README.md)

Use 512 new pairs at generator indices 16000–16511. For each host destination, compute an activation difference only from the corresponding two donor runs. Translate it through the 25-dimensional map and add it to the host token state before the remaining blocks. Neither the partner's host features nor the candidate answer identity enters that translated change. A 25-dimensional orthogonal random direction at exactly the same injected norm is the matched control. Same-model full-token exchange and the original host are the positive and clean controls. The random control draws once per direction with a fixed seed and uses no answer identities. Use the original pretrained checkpoints and cuts after blocks 6 and 10.

Require at least 100 initially solved pairs, full-site reversal on at least 80% of those pairs, learned-map reversal on at least 80%, and an advantage of at least 20 percentage points above the norm-matched random control with a positive lower paired 95% interval. That would demonstrate transfer of a task-specific activation difference between pretrained networks at a known lexical source. It would not transfer a complete module. Report both 0-to-1 and 1-to-0 preference switches, norm ratios and the native control. Allow a few minutes, based on the earlier subspace runs.
