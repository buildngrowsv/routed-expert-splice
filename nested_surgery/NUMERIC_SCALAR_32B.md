# Can a single answer-free calibration recover the borrowed 32B edit?

The raw 14B interface reversed 92 of 507 pairs in the 32B host. Matching its edit norm to the fitted 32B interface reversed 414 on the same panel. The raw edit had a median of 64.35% of the matched norm. Test one scale determined from activation differences, without fitting recipient directions or using answer labels.

Use an independent set of 128 paired direct-return functions at indices 123000–123127. The two variants differ at their assignment digit. Measure the Euclidean distance between the full native assignment states after block 10 in the 14B checkpoint and block 13 in the 32B checkpoint. Set the gain to the median of the 32B distance divided by the 14B distance across calibration pairs. Use no arithmetic labels, logits, recipient fitting, or held-out outcomes to select the gain. Apply this scalar to the archived 14B affine edit in the 32B receiver. Match the resulting norm using the original 14B orthogonal directions.

Score the exact earlier 512 held-out pairs against the full vocabulary. Use the intersection of native-correct pairs in the new run and both historical controls. Compare the scalar-gain arm with the raw arm, the recipient-fitted norm match, and the new random control. Require at least 100 shared native-correct pairs, at least 75% reversal with the scalar, a paired lower bound of at least +20 points above raw borrowing, and a paired lower bound no worse than −10 points versus the norm-matched historical arm. Archive the calibration prompts, distances, gain and per-pair ranks. Estimate four minutes.
