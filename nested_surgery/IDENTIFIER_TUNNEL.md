# Can the learned identifier subspace serve as a fixed forward reference?

The fitted 25-dimensional letter subspace reversed every fresh counterfactual pair in two frozen Qwen models. Use the same independently recoverable subspace as a tunnel instead of applying one edit and letting it evolve. Keep its 25 coordinates at a single declaration token fixed for seven following blocks, then release the constraint. Compare the two same-size subspaces from the preceding test. The learned directions come from 512 calibration pairs. The random directions are orthogonal and use a counterfactual initial displacement matched in norm. [Learned subspace and control](identifier_subspace_study/README.md)

In separate arms preserve the prompt’s initial projection or preserve the counterfactual partner projection. The rest of each token’s state continues through the model normally. For the random counterfactual match the learned edit’s initial norm and then preserve that perturbed random projection. The unmodified transformer is the clean benchmark. Use 512 fresh program pairs (indices 12000–12511), both qualified checkpoints, and cuts after blocks 6 and 10 respectively. Require the learned clean reference to preserve at least 90% of pairs solved by the original model, the wrong learned reference to reverse at least 80% of those retained pairs, and a reversal advantage of at least 20 percentage points over the random reference among pairs both clean interventions preserve, with a positive paired lower bound. If fewer than 100 pairs remain eligible for that last comparison, report the clean breakdown. This asks whether freezing a known task direction is useful under an explicit intervention. The experiment isolates one source location and one known fact and uses no transformer updates. Allow about six minutes on the Studio.
